When Do You Need a White Background?

White backgrounds are required or strongly recommended in several common situations:

  • Amazon product listings. Amazon's main image guidelines require a pure white background (RGB 255, 255, 255) with the product filling at least 85% of the frame. Non-compliant images are suppressed from search results.
  • eBay and Shopify listings. Both platforms recommend white backgrounds for product images to improve click-through rates. Studies consistently show white-background product photos convert better than lifestyle shots on category pages.
  • Passport and visa photos. Most country requirements specify a white or off-white background. The US, UK, and EU all mandate plain white or light grey backgrounds for official ID photos.
  • Professional headshots. LinkedIn profile photos and company directories often request clean, neutral backgrounds. White is the most universally accepted choice.
  • Print-on-demand and merchandise. Platforms like Printful and Redbubble recommend white backgrounds for mockup accuracy — the white area becomes transparent when the design is applied to a product.

White Background vs. Transparent Background — Which Do You Need?

These two are often confused. Here's the practical difference:

  • White background (JPG or PNG): The background is a solid white fill. Use this for Amazon/eBay listings, passport photos, and any situation where the specification says "white background." Most e-commerce platforms require JPG, which cannot store transparency — white is the correct choice.
  • Transparent background (PNG only): The background has no color and shows through whatever sits beneath it in a design or webpage. Use this for logos, icons, and graphics that will be placed on different colored surfaces.

If you need a transparent background instead, use the Remove Background tool to get a PNG with a transparent layer.

Tips for the Best Results

  • Shoot against a contrasting background. If your subject is light-colored, photograph it against a dark surface (and vice versa). This makes it much easier for the AI to detect edges cleanly.
  • Center the subject and avoid touching edges. Keep the product or person away from the frame borders. Subjects that bleed off the edge are harder to isolate accurately.
  • Use even lighting. Harsh shadows that fall on the background can be mistaken for part of the subject. Soft, even lighting produces the cleanest cuts.
  • Check fine details. After conversion, zoom in on hair, fur, or transparent areas (like glass). If edges look rough, the Change Background tool lets you fine-tune the result.
  • Verify your white is true white. For Amazon compliance, the background must be RGB 255,255,255 — not off-white or light grey. Use the Image Quality Checker to confirm the output meets platform specifications.
